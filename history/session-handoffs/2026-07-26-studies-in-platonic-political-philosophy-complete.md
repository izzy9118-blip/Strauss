## Seven foundational results

1. **Nomos vs. Physis** — law is indispensable but not self-justifying; natural right, human law, and revealed divine Law remain distinct.
2. **Philosophy vs. Poetry** — philosophy seeks the whole, while poetry, drama, history, rhetoric, and symbol disclose and form souls; neither may absorb the other.
3. **Theory vs. Practice** — knowledge does not automatically become rule, and success does not establish truth; prudence, authority, institutions, communication, and chance mediate action.
4. **The Theologico-Political Problem** — autonomous inquiry and obedient revelation retain rival comprehensive claims while political society must act before the conflict is resolved.
5. **Athens vs. Jerusalem** — wonder, nature, and understanding remain opposed at the highest level to fear of the Lord, creation, command, and obedient love.
6. **The Wise vs. The Vulgar** — political society needs superior judgment but cannot certify wisdom through office, title, success, persecution, secrecy, scholarship, election, or self-description.
7. **Ancients vs. Moderns** — modernity gains reliability and power partly by lowering or narrowing the ancient and biblical horizon; the gain is real and the human cost remains open.

## Governing interpretive result

Speech and deed is preserved as a shared interpretive discipline rather than an eighth foundational problem. Every claim must be examined through speaker, audience, occasion, wording, action, outcome, arrangement, omission, source use, evidentiary status, and documentary layer.

The Thucydides finding remains the strongest structural exemplar: the concentration of fully dramatized speech around Melos and Sicily, followed by the absence of fully quoted speeches in Book VIII, may elevate the Sicilian catastrophe. Incompletion remains possible, so structural evidence is not converted into demonstrated authorial intention.

## Strict theologico-political result

> Philosophy cannot surrender autonomous inquiry to an authority it has not examined. Revelation cannot surrender divine sovereignty to autonomous human reason without ceasing to be revelation. Political society cannot wait for the conflict to be resolved, yet its need for authoritative law cannot turn opinion into knowledge.

The collection sharpens three different attempted mediations:

- **Maimonides:** preserves articulated differences among knowledge, belief, prophecy, legal presumption, Mosaic Law, and practice.
- **Machiavelli:** subordinates religion to political use as civil theology.
- **Cohen:** harmonizes reason and revelation by transforming revelation into rational moral constitution.

None is treated as a demonstrated final resolution.
