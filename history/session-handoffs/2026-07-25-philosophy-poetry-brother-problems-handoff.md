## 7. Central interpretive result

The completed book establishes the following governing result:

> Poetry is superior to truncated natural philosophy where philosophy lacks self-knowledge, prudence, knowledge of souls, Eros, music, audience, and political consequence. Mature Socratic philosophy is superior to poetry only by integrating the truths poetry discloses into inquiry into the all-comprehensive whole.

This superiority is **integrative, not presumptive**.

The young Aristophanic Socrates is defective because he is represented as:

- self-forgetting;
- politically imprudent;
- insufficiently attentive to the city;
- ignorant of differentiated souls;
- unerotic;
- amusic;
- unable to judge pupils;
- unable to protect his teaching or himself;
- insufficiently aware of what gods, law, family, music, and authoritative opinion do for human beings.

The mature Platonic-Xenophontic Socrates answers the accusation by joining inquiry into the whole with:

- practical wisdom;
- political responsibility;
- lawfulness in deed;
- knowledge and guidance of souls;
- Eros;
- music;
- patience;
- urbanity;
- differentiated education.

The historical claim that Aristophanes caused Socrates' transformation remains open and unproven. A supported structural response must not be converted into an unsupported claim of historical causation.

The shared origin is:

> Both problems arise when philosophy challenges the authoritative poetic-theological horizon through which political society understands gods, divine law, justice, piety, education, and the highest life.

The shared question is:

> Who can speak with wisdom and authority concerning the gods, justice, education, and the highest human life?

The distinction must remain exact:

### Philosophy vs. Poetry owns

- rival human forms of wisdom;
- poetic and philosophic education;
- imitation and dramatic representation;
- comedy and tragedy;
- knowledge of souls;
- Eros and music;
- audience and rhetoric;
- poetic guidance and the poetic correction of philosophy;
- the philosophic integration or critique of poetic knowledge.

### The Theologico-Political Problem owns

- ultimate authority among autonomous reason and divine revelation;
- divine command;
- prophecy;
- sacred law;
- theology;
- political sovereignty;
- the limits of rational refutation of revelation;
- the authority of an acknowledged or claimed divine disclosure.

### Non-absorption rule

- poetry is not revelation;
- revelation is not poetic construction;
- civic theology is not identical with divine truth;
- comic overthrow of a god is not proof against revelation;
- philosophic criticism of civic gods is not a completed refutation of divine authority;
- neither brother may reproduce the other's full doctrine.

Both problems must coactivate when inquiry concerns:

- gods;
- piety;
- prophecy;
- oath;
- sacred law;
- worship;
- divine mediation;
- resistance to divine command;
- the human recognition of divine authority;

## 9. Permanent findings from *Socrates and Aristophanes*

The following findings are ready to govern continued inquiry, while remaining subject to later corpus testing:

1. Poetry and philosophy appear as rival forms of wisdom.
2. Comedy possesses substantive knowledge of human types, desire, Eros, music, suffering, justice, piety, imitation, audience, and political consequence.
3. A character's statement, a chorus's judgment, or a dramatic victory must not automatically be identified with the poet's doctrine.
4. Comedy is total within its dramatic form but remains a partial mirror of life.
5. Poetry may know souls deeply without possessing final knowledge of the highest end.
6. Philosophy's superiority requires integration of poetic truth rather than dismissal of poetry.
7. Knowledge of souls and of the soul belongs within knowledge of the whole.
8. Education frequently redirects governing desire through authority, image, reward, fear, hope, ritual, and altered circumstances rather than eradicating it.
9. Religious performance, initiation, or pious emotion does not by itself establish knowledge of the divine object.
10. Human recognition of gods, divine authority, and sacred law must be studied through the soul that fears, loves, obeys, represents, or resists them.
11. Knowledge of how gods function for human beings does not settle divine truth.
12. Poetic theology must remain distinct from revelation.
13. Philosophy cannot responsibly judge divine authority while ignoring the erotic, poetic, political, and sacred orientation of the soul.
14. Poetic power may preserve or replace a public theology more effectively than natural inquiry can abolish the human need for authoritative orientation.
15. Strauss's use of comedy requires comparison of explicit speech with dramatic deed, outcome, omission, and demonstrated rank.

Permanent interpretive key:

> Strauss's comedy corrects solemn scholarship.

This does not authorize mechanical inversion. Comedy must be examined through speaker, audience, occasion, action, omission, consequence, self-implication, and the distinction between genuine and merely claimed rank.
