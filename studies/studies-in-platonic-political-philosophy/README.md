It records what the source supports, distinguishes Strauss's essays from editorial framing, preserves unresolved alternatives, and prepares problem-specific integration without silently certifying a final doctrine.

## Documentary boundary

The collection is a qualified primary Strauss corpus witness. Strauss chose the title and arrangement, but the book was published posthumously. Joseph Cropsey's Foreword and Thomas L. Pangle's Introduction are editorial or secondary contextual witnesses. Pangle states that Strauss had not begun the intended introduction, and the projected essay on Plato's *Gorgias* was not completed. Editorial reconstruction may guide questions but may not govern Strauss's meaning.

The repository study is grounded in the reviewed PDF witness identified in `source-status.yaml`. The source text is not reproduced here. Findings remain revisable through edition comparison and direct reconstruction of the authors Strauss interprets.

## Governing safeguards

1. Strauss's essays govern claims about Strauss's arguments; they do not replace Plato, Thucydides, Xenophon, Aristotle, the Bible, Maimonides, Nietzsche, Machiavelli, Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Kant, Burke, Talmon, Macpherson, or Cohen as independent witnesses.
2. Poetry is not revelation, and revelation is not poetic construction.
3. Civic theology and politically useful religion are not automatically genuine revelation.
4. Philosophy's inability to refute revelation does not prove a particular revelation.
5. Political necessity, historical survival, suffering, success, office, scholarship, secrecy, or self-description does not certify wisdom or truth.
6. Structural evidence, sequence, omission, silence, and numerical correspondence may support interpretation but do not independently prove deliberate hidden intention.
7. Speaker, audience, occasion, action, outcome, source use, and evidentiary status must remain distinct.
8. The Bible and Torah may contain multiple source, compilation, and editorial layers; intention must be assigned to the correct documentary level.
9. The posthumous arrangement may be examined as an illuminating whole, but every transition may not be attributed automatically to a single completed authorial plan.
10. No problem object, witness, source, predecessor, or historical artifact is revised or certified merely by this study's completion.

## Principal result

The collection does not abolish the foundational conflicts. It preserves political philosophy as disciplined inquiry into law, nature, poetry, practice, revelation, wisdom, history, and modernity while refusing to convert political necessity into truth or philosophical uncertainty into revealed certainty.

Its strict theologico-political result is:

> Philosophy cannot surrender autonomous inquiry to an authority it has not examined. Revelation cannot surrender divine sovereignty to autonomous human reason without ceasing to be revelation. Political society cannot wait for the conflict to be resolved, yet its need for authoritative law cannot turn opinion into knowledge.

## Completion limit

This study completes the Strauss-source reconstruction at collection level. It does not complete the independent primary-text reconstructions required for Plato, Thucydides, Xenophon, Aristotle, biblical revelation, Maimonides, Nietzsche, Machiavelli, Hobbes, Cohen, or the other authors and historical claims encountered in the volume.
